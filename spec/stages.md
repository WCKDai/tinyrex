# TinyRex stage briefs

These are the briefs an AI agent was given, one per stage, to build TinyRex in Polykrom. Each stage is a fresh
headless session that opens the design the previous stage saved. The source of truth is [`stages.py`](stages.py),
which the harness reads; this file is generated from it for reading.

Placeholders the harness fills in: `{input}` (the previous stage's design), `{save}` (this stage's design), and the
output files `{gif}`, `{png}`, `{bom}`, `{mjcf}` and `{polished}`.

## Rules common to every stage

Appended to every brief:

> Units are millimetres and degrees; Z is up; the ground is the plane z = 0 and TinyRex faces +X. Work only through the Polykrom tools. Standard hardware and actuators come from the parts library: parts.library lists the families (servo, socket_head_cap_screw, hex_nut, washer, bearing...) with their dimensions and ratings. An actuator inserted with parts.insert reports its `output` origin (the axis its horn turns about) and its `output_part`, the horn, a separate part: a link driven by the servo is joined rigidly to the horn, and the joint that turns it is a revolute joint about `output`. Everything has to make physical sense: every part is held by something (a fastener, a joint, a seat), nothing floats, and nothing overlaps another part except fasteners in their holes. Parts a real build would 3D-print are their own components in PLA; bought parts are library parts or, where the library has none, envelopes at their real size and mass (give an envelope a material whose density makes it weigh its real mass). Make it look like a T-rex, not a box: use fillets, chamfers, lofts or revolves for the printed shells. Colours, Polykrom's (appearance.assign): printed body shells lime (#CCFF00), armour plates, dorsal spikes and claws black (#111111), teeth off-white (#EDEDED), actuators in their own colours. Ground nothing: TinyRex will stand on its feet. Save your progress to {save} after each major part, not only at the end, so nothing is lost if the session ends early. When the stage is done and you have verified it, save the design to {save} and reply with a short report: what you did, the numbers you verified, and anything that did not work or that you had to work around.

## 1. Torso (`s1_torso`)

Start a new design: TinyRex, a small bipedal T-rex robot in the class of Pollen Robotics' Microduck and the Open Duck Mini, its joints driven by Feetech STS3215 serial servos (library family servo, size STS3215). This stage is the torso. A printed torso shell shaped like a T-rex's chest and belly, about 130 long (X), 90 wide (Y) and 80 high, hollow with 2.5 mm walls, a black 2 mm armour plate screwed on top with M3 screws into printed bosses, and a row of five black dorsal spikes on the plate. Inside: a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W (envelope 65 x 30 x 5 mm, 11 g, on four printed standoffs with M2.5 screws through its holes, 58 x 23 mm apart), a BNO085 IMU board (envelope 25 x 18 x 4 mm, 3 g, on two M2.5 standoffs) and a 2S 1000 mAh LiPo (envelope 70 x 35 x 18 mm, 65 g) held by a printed strap screwed to the shell. Under the torso, two STS3215 for the hip yaw joints, their output axes vertical (pointing down), 50 mm apart in Y, each on printed mounts with M2 screws (library) into its mounting holes; the bottom of the torso shell about 210 mm above the ground. On the left flank (+Y), Polykrom's pixel p: a printed badge of 3 mm square cells, 6 cells tall, rows from the top '#####.', '##..##', '##..##', '#####.', '##....', '##....' (# is a cell), black, standing out 1.5 mm. Name the torso's component Torso. Report the torso's size, its mass with the two hip servos, and how every part is held.

## 2. Left leg (`s2_leg`)

Open {input} (TinyRex's torso with two hip yaw servos underneath). Build the left leg (+Y), every part held, a 6-joint leg so TinyRex can walk statically: on the left hip yaw servo's horn a printed bracket carrying an STS3215 for hip roll (output axis along X); on its horn a bracket carrying an STS3215 for hip pitch (axis along Y); on its horn a printed thigh shaped like a dinosaur's thigh, 75 mm from the hip pitch axis to the knee axis, carrying an STS3215 for the knee (axis Y); on the knee horn a printed shin 75 mm long carrying an STS3215 for ankle pitch (axis Y) and, on its horn, a bracket with an STS3215 for ankle roll (axis X); on that horn a big printed T-rex foot: a flat sole about 100 long and 60 wide, three toes forward, each tipped with a black claw, and a 2 mm black TPU pad under the sole. Revolute joints about each servo's `output` origin: L_hip_yaw (-25..25), L_hip_roll (-25..25), L_hip_pitch (-90..90), L_knee (0..130 or -130..0, bending the knee forward), L_ankle_pitch (-60..60), L_ankle_roll (-25..25), each turning its own component; servos screwed to what holds them, links joined rigidly to the horns. At zero, the leg is straight down and the sole is flat. Then move the whole TinyRex so the sole's underside is on z = 0 (nothing is grounded). Verify: sweep each joint through its limits (joint.sweep) and fix any collision; report the sole's height range and the foot's reach, and the leg's mass.

## 3. Right leg (`s3_legs`)

Open {input} (TinyRex: torso and a complete left leg, joints L_hip_yaw, L_hip_roll, L_hip_pitch, L_knee, L_ankle_pitch, L_ankle_roll). Build the right leg as a mirror of the left on the right hip yaw servo: joints R_hip_yaw, R_hip_roll, R_hip_pitch, R_knee, R_ankle_pitch, R_ankle_roll with the same limits, so the same joint values give mirror-image poses (a real servo can't be mirrored: place library servos turned so their geometry matches the mirror image). Narrow the hip roll limits on both legs if needed so the two legs can't collide (sweep both legs' hip roll and report the closest gap). Check that every part of the right leg is held, that each of the 12 leg joints moves only its own leg's parts, and that nothing overlaps at zero (inspect.interference). Report the total mass, the centre of mass and the 12-joint table.

## 5. Standing pose and statics (`s5_stand`)

Open {input} (TinyRex: 17 joints, 12 in the legs). Make it stand: pick joint values for a standing pose with the knees slightly bent, the torso level, both soles flat on z = 0 (move the whole robot so they touch; nothing is grounded), the feet under the hips, and the head and tail balancing each other so the centre of mass sits over the middle of the two feet. Then verify it as a machine with inspect.statics (support: the two soles, ground 0): the mass, the centre of mass, whether it lies over the support polygon and the stability margin, and every joint's gravity torque against its servo's rating; every utilisation should be below 50% of stall. Also check the single-support case that walking will need: with the centre of mass moved over one foot (by hip and ankle roll), is it inside that one foot's polygon, and by how much? Change the pose or the design if anything fails, and say what you changed. Check that nothing overlaps in the pose. Save the pose as the first step of a motion study named Stand, and report the numbers.

## 6. Walking gait and MuJoCo (`s6_walk`)

Open {input} (TinyRex standing with both soles on z = 0). Make it walk with a statically stable gait: a motion study named Walk of at least 200 steps, keyed only on TinyRex's joints, in which it takes two steps forward (left, then right), each about 40 mm long with the swing foot rising about 20 mm, while the stance foot stays flat on the ground without sliding. Before each step, shift the weight over the stance foot with the hip and ankle roll joints, and swing the tail toward the stance side to help; keep the centre of mass inside the stance foot's polygon throughout the single-support phase. Let the head bob and the jaw snap once per step for character. Work out the joint angles from the leg geometry (inverse kinematics), end in the starting pose so the study loops. Verify: inspect.statics over the study (support: the two soles, ground 0), the smallest stability margin (must stay above 5 mm) and the highest joint utilisation; no interference across the study (motion.interference); the stance feet don't slide (trace them). Then check it in physics: sim.mujoco with study Walk, cycles 2 (MuJoCo with the base free; it reports how far TinyRex walked, its drift and tilt, whether it fell, and each servo's peak force against its rating). If it falls or drifts badly, change the gait (slower weight shifts, a wider stance, more time in double support) and run it again; report each attempt. Export the study as a GIF to {gif} (900 x 600). Report the margin, the utilisations, the step length, the foot clearance and the MuJoCo result.

## 8. Design polish (in progress) (`s8_polish`)

Open {input} (TinyRex, complete: 17 joints, Stand and Walk studies, verified). This stage is a design polish pass, in the way an industrial designer refines a prototype into a product: make it look finished, premium and fierce, while keeping everything that works. Work in passes. Each pass:

1. Look at it. Take screenshots from four views: a front three-quarter from slightly below (hero), a side, a top, and a back three-quarter.

2. Critique it honestly against these rules: no raw boxes on visible printed parts; every exposed edge of a printed part rounded or chamfered within one consistent family of radii (about 1, 2.5 and 5 mm by part size); organic forms (skull, tail, thighs, chest) read as smooth sculpted shells, not prisms; bosses and ribs blend into walls; the jaw, teeth, brow and spikes give it attitude; part lines and colour blocks (lime shells, black armour, plates, claws, off-white teeth) are crisp and deliberate; nothing looks unfinished.

3. If inspect.polish is available (list_commands), run it for the printed bodies and use its sharp-edge list, suggested radii and fixes.

4. Make the five changes with the most visual impact. Where solid.fillet refuses an edge (curved edges often fail), get the round another way: rebuild that part from a loft or revolve through rounded sections, chamfer instead, or add a sculpted cover. Never change a joint's axis, a servo mount or a horn interface, and keep every part held.

SILHOUETTE FIRST. Before edge rounding, fix the big shapes, which matter far more than radii. Today TinyRex reads as an upright stack of exposed black servo boxes, more giraffe than T-rex. Make it read as a T-rex from 3 m away: (a) printed lime fairings over the exposed servo stacks on thighs and shins, shaped as a muscular drumstick thigh and a slender shin, each fixed to the link it rides on (screws into the bracket), clear of its neighbours over the whole joint range, leaving horns and servo cable exits free; (b) a sculpted neck sleeve over the neck servos; (c) hide the blue SG90 jaw servo behind cheek covers so the mouth shows only teeth; (d) a thicker, longer tapering tail that reads as the counterweight, carrying the spike line; (e) a chest that leans forward so torso, neck and head read as a forward-reaching line, and a standing head pitch with the snout forward, not up. Lofts through rounded sections are the right tool for fairings and the tail; solid.shell gives them walls. Keep added mass under about 250 g total, and keep the joints, servo mounts and horn interfaces untouched. After the silhouette pass re-run the interference check over the Walk study, statics on Stand and Walk (stability margin at least 5 mm) and sim.mujoco on Walk at 0.08 s per step; if the walk falls, move mass or trim the tail until it holds. Then do the edge-rounding passes. Do at least two passes, more while the look clearly improves. Save to {save} after every pass. At the end:

- re-check that nothing overlaps (inspect.interference) and that every joint still sweeps through its limits;

- run statics on the Stand pose and sim.mujoco on the Walk study (2 cycles) again, and report if anything got worse;

- render the hero view (render.preflight, render.start, render.status, render.export) to {polished} at 1600 x 1000;

- report before and after for each pass: what you changed, the inspect.polish scores if available, and what you could not do because the kernel refused.
